What sets bitdispatcher apart

Few dispatch systems have all of these. bitdispatcher has every one, in the same free program, on the dispatcher's own screen. Each picture below is the real screen. Each says how far it has been tested: live against a real call-handling system in a lab, or with the simulator. None of it is certified yet.

NG9-1-1 from the ground up

Calls from the ESInet with their location (PIDF-LO), the caller ID's STIR/SHAKEN check, the address checked against the county's address points, rebids, the EIDO, ESRP routing and each PSAP's queue state. Text to 911, real-time text and TTY both ways.

Tested live with BitVoice

Live video from the caller

One click texts the caller a link; their phone's camera streams into the call. It starts blurred, so nobody sees a scene before choosing to.

Tested live with BitVoice (a test camera)

Photos kept as evidence

A photo shows its facts first: when the camera took it, where, and its SHA-256 hash. It is shown only when someone chooses to, every look is recorded, and one click holds it as evidence.

Tested live with BitVoice: the hash matches

Transcripts, with the details pulled out

The caller's words written out as they speak, and what matters lifted out of them: a weapon, an address, a vehicle, a plate, which way they went. After the call, the recording's summary.

Tested live with BitVoice's speech-to-text

The caller's language

A Spanish caller translated line by line, and replies the dispatcher picks in English and the caller hears in Spanish, read out or texted. An agency's own language model can take on the rest.

Tested with the simulator (a built-in emergency phrasebook)

Vehicle crash data, rated

A car's crash notification arrives before anyone calls: speed, rollover, how many inside, an electric car. It is rated by field-triage criteria, with what to send.

Tested with the simulator's crash notifications

A record that cannot quietly change

Every action is an event carrying the hash of the one before it. Change, remove or reorder one and the chain breaks, which anyone can check from the browser. Corrections are new events, never edits.

A training board of its own

Scripted scenarios on the agency's own map and units, scored, never touching the live board; shared exercises where an instructor injects callers and radio traffic.

And the everyday work, done reliably

Everything a small or mid-size 911 centre needs, done reliably

The large CAD vendors price out the counties and cities that need dispatch the most. bitdispatcher is one program, free, with its source: no server farm, no database to stand up first, no per-seat licence.

New

The rest of what a big CAD does, now built in

What large CAD systems commonly have and bitdispatcher did not, built and tested in the browser this month. Each has a chapter in the manual and can be tried on the demo.

  1. Second and third alarms: one command sends the next level of the run card
  2. Crews and staffing: who is on each unit this shift, and a medic with no paramedic is not sent as ALS
  3. Paging volunteer and off-duty responders, who answer from a link: responding, to the scene, or not available
  4. Fire-ground accountability: roll-call (PAR) timers, crews reported missing, benchmarks
  5. Tow rotation for police, fair by class and zone
  6. Scheduled calls and special events
  7. A contacts directory and on-call lists, on each incident's call-out list
  8. Geofences: alerts when a unit enters or leaves an area, cautions inside one
  9. Each unit's status history for any day
  10. Times and details sent to the ambulance crews' patient reports (NEMSIS 3.5)
  11. Warrants and civil process, flagged at the address
  12. Alerting the public: shelter in place or evacuate an area, as CAP to the IPAWS profile, with two-person approval
  13. Agencies configured one by one, with each person's coverage loading at sign-in and per-agency publish and rollback
  14. Saved duty presets that follow the person to any console, locked until deliberately changed
  15. Several working calls in tabs beside call entry, kept on the server
  16. A messages and BOLO inbox with four priorities, attachments, versions and receipts
  17. One search that says why each result matched, and a unit recall timeline
  18. Who is on duty and on each unit, and capability from the qualified people aboard
  19. Key profiles, including a PremierOne-style layout, and one-line F7 traffic stops and F11 statuses

Paging is tested against stand-ins for Twilio, ntfy, email and webhooks; the demo uses a simulated pager. Public alerts are checked with Google's CAP library and its IPAWS profile and with EAS Station's feed reader, and the NIBRS file with SEARCH's validator; none of that is certification.
